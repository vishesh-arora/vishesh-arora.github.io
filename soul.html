The Problem

Users wanted to talk. The experience wasn't ready for them.

FRND is an audio social platform where users come primarily for conversations. But for new users, the experience had a few critical gaps that hurt both onboarding and retention.

First conversation friction

Talking to a stranger felt awkward and intimidating, especially for new users just finding their footing on the platform.

Supply-demand mismatch

Conversations depended on another user being available. During peak hours, early mornings, and festive periods, this broke down frequently.

Inconsistent experience

Quality of conversation varied wildly depending on who was online. There was no reliable floor for what a new user would experience.

The combined effect: new users who couldn't find someone to talk to — or found the experience too intimidating — dropped off early, never reaching the engagement that makes FRND sticky.

The Solution

Soul — an AI voice companion that's always there.

We built Soul: an AI-powered voice companion designed to make conversations instant, natural, and available 24/7. The idea was simple — give new users a safe, non-judgmental space to start talking, with no dependency on another human being online.

Reduce first-conversation friction

Start with a warm, forgiving AI before talking to real users — lowering the barrier for new users significantly.

Always-on availability

No more empty waiting rooms. Soul is available at any hour — peak traffic or 3am — removing supply-demand failure entirely.

Consistent quality

By controlling the conversational experience end-to-end, we could ensure a reliable, warm, and engaging interaction every time.

Tech Stack

A real-time voice pipeline built for low latency.

The architecture needed to be fast — voice interactions are unforgiving when it comes to response time. We chose Sarvam for both STT and TTS given its strong performance on Indian-accented speech and regional language support, which is core to FRND's user base.

Key Challenges

Nothing worked perfectly on day one.

1 Conversation quality felt robotic

Early responses were generic, flat, and didn't feel like a real companion. Users could immediately sense they were talking to a machine.

Fix: Continuous prompt iteration + adding rolling message context to make responses feel aware and natural.

2 Users dropping early in conversations

A significant number of users were abandoning within the first few messages — the critical window where the product needed to hook them.

Fix: Message-level drop-off analysis, improving the first 3 responses specifically, and adding AI-initiated messages to keep conversation going.

3 Safety and moderation

An AI voice companion on a social platform needed robust guardrails — users would inevitably test boundaries or find themselves in sensitive conversations.

Fix: Guardrails baked into the system prompt + a separate moderation layer for flagging sensitive topics before they reached the LLM response.

4 Latency in voice responses

Any perceptible lag in a voice conversation breaks the immersion entirely. The STT → LLM → TTS pipeline had to be optimised end-to-end.

Fix: Worked closely with engineering to profile and reduce latency at each step of the pipeline.

Key Learnings

What building Soul taught me.

  • In conversational products, communication is the product. Tone, pacing, and word choice are as critical as any feature decision.
  • Small changes in response style can have outsized retention impact — what feels like a detail to an engineer is the entire experience for a user.
  • Combining qualitative review (listening to actual conversations) with data signals (message-level drop-offs) is the only reliable iteration loop for LLM products.
